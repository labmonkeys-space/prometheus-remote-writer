[#operations]
= Operations

== Self-metrics

The plugin exposes internal operational metrics via a Dropwizard registry
and prints them through the Karaf shell command
`opennms:prometheus-writer-stats`.

=== Throughput and drops

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Counter                                    | What it counts

| `samples_written_total`                    | Samples successfully delivered to the endpoint.
| `samples_dropped_4xx_total`                | Samples in batches the endpoint rejected with 4xx.
| `samples_dropped_5xx_total`                | Samples in batches that exhausted the 5xx retry budget.
| `samples_dropped_transport_total`          | IOException / socket failures (distinct from 5xx so you can alert separately).
| `samples_unconfirmed_by_receiver_total`    | Samples in requests the backend answered 2xx while its own `X-Prometheus-Remote-Write-Samples-Written` header reported writing fewer. Remote Write 2.0 forbids a 2xx on a partial write, so a non-zero value means the backend contradicted its acknowledgement and some of those samples may be missing there; a WARN names the counts. The samples are already in `samples_written_total`, so this is not a term of the reconciliation identity. A backend that does not send the header never moves it.
| `samples_dropped_queue_full_total`         | Samples refused because the in-memory queue was full and no disk tier was configured to take them. Only ever non-zero with `overflow.max-size-bytes = 0`: with a disk tier a full queue spills instead of refusing, and refusals appear in `samples_dropped_overflow_full_total`. Counted in samples per refused `store()` attempt: under `queue.store-policy = partial` each refused sample, under `all-or-nothing` every mapped sample of a refused call. With Horizon's default ring-buffer writer, which does not retry, this is the number of samples lost. The offheap writer retries the whole call, so its retries are counted again. Earlier releases counted one per failed call.
| `samples_spilled_total`                    | Samples written to a shard's disk bucket because its memory queue had no room. Includes the whole memory backlog moved at the transition into spilling, so the first spill of a burst adds up to `queue.capacity / writer.shards` at once, not 1.
| `samples_drained_from_overflow_total`      | Samples read back off a bucket and accepted by the backend. Over a quiet period `rate(samples_spilled_total) - rate(samples_drained_from_overflow_total)` is how fast the tier is filling or draining.
| `samples_dropped_overflow_full_total`      | Samples refused because a shard's memory queue and its bucket were both full, under `overflow.full = refuse`. These are lost, and `store()` threw for the call carrying them.
| `samples_evicted_overflow_total`           | Samples discarded by evicting a bucket's oldest segment under `overflow.full = drop-oldest`. The newest data is kept at the cost of the oldest; the segment is the eviction unit, so this moves in steps rather than one at a time.
| `samples_dropped_nonfinite_total`          | Samples whose value was `NaN`, `+Inf`, or `-Inf`.
| `samples_dropped_duplicate_total`          | Same-timestamp same-series dedup (last-write-wins).
| `samples_unparseable_resource_id_total`    | Samples whose `resourceId` matched none of the parser grammars (rising rate ⇒ open an issue).
| `samples_synthesized_mtype_total`          | Reads where the Prometheus response lacked an `mtype` label and the plugin synthesized `mtype="gauge"` to keep graphs renderable. Ticks once per Metric reconstruction — per matched series in `findMetrics`, once per fetch in `getTimeSeriesData`. See <<mtype-roundtrip>>. Should drop to flat once Prometheus retention has aged out pre-fix data, or rise indefinitely if `labels.exclude = mtype` is configured.
| `store_calls_total`                        | Calls to `store()`. With `store_calls_failed_total` this is the caller-side failure rate that #154 had to borrow from OpenNMS's own timers.
| `store_calls_failed_total`                 | `store()` calls that threw. Under `queue.store-policy = partial` a call that was partially accepted still counts as failed.
| `store_samples_offered_total`              | Samples handed to `store()` before label mapping, plus the metadata samples the plugin emits itself (see <<resource-metadata>>). Over a window, `offered = written + samples_dropped_unmapped_total + Σ dropped_* + queue_depth + overflow_pending_samples`. It is exact between scrapes rather than at every instant: depth and pending are read per shard without a global lock, so a scrape taken mid-spill can miss a sample in flight between the two. It also does not hold across a restart, since a recovered bucket writes in the new process what was offered in the old.
| `samples_dropped_shutdown_total`           | Samples that had left the queue (built, or in the builder's hand) when a forced shutdown interrupted the flusher before they could be sent. Non-zero only when `shutdown.grace-period-ms` expired.
| `samples_dropped_unmapped_total`           | Samples the label mapper could not map because the metric had no name. Never offered to a queue or a bucket. Only a non-OpenNMS caller of the TimeSeriesStorage API produces these.
| `http_write_duration_ms_total`             | Wall milliseconds spent inside remote-write HTTP calls, retries and backoff included. Mean round-trip: `rate(http_write_duration_ms_total) / rate(http_writes_successful_total + http_writes_failed_total)`. Sender utilisation per shard: `rate(http_write_duration_ms_total) / (1000 × writer.shards)`; the builder's share is `flusher_build_ms_total`. Samples per write: `rate(samples_written_total) / rate(http_writes_successful_total)`.
| `sample_latency_ms_total`                  | Sum over written samples of the time from `store()` to backend acknowledgement, in milliseconds. Mean end-to-end latency: `rate(sample_latency_ms_total) / rate(samples_written_total)`. This is the number a latency budget is stated in; it includes queue wait, linger, build, the round-trip, and for spilled samples the time on disk, counted from the original `store()` even across a restart.
| `store_call_duration_ms_total`             | Wall milliseconds OpenNMS's writer threads spent inside `store()`. Mean call time: `rate(store_call_duration_ms_total) / rate(store_calls_total)`. A rising mean is the first sign the plugin is holding the persister, which shows on the OpenNMS side as ring-buffer drops.
| `flusher_build_ms_total`                   | Milliseconds queue-mode flushers spent building requests (grouping, protobuf, snappy). Build and HTTP now overlap per shard, so per-shard builder utilisation is `rate(flusher_build_ms_total) / (1000 × writer.shards)` and sender utilisation is `rate(http_write_duration_ms_total) / (1000 × writer.shards)`; headroom is whichever is lower.
| `http_writes_4xx_total`, `http_writes_5xx_total`, `http_writes_transport_total` | Failed writes by cause, as request counts. Error rate: their sum over `http_writes_successful_total + http_writes_failed_total`. The `samples_dropped_*` counters carry the same failures in samples.
| `http_write_duration_bucket_le_{5,10,25,50,100,250,1000,inf}` | Cumulative count of writes whose wall time was at or below the bound in milliseconds, retries and backoff included. The exporter rule below maps them to a Prometheus histogram, so `histogram_quantile` works on the backend's round-trip; a bimodal backend shows here and not in the mean.
| `flusher_linger_ms_total`                  | Milliseconds queue-mode flushers spent waiting for a batch to fill after a first sample arrived (`batch.linger-ms`). Not idle: there was something to send. Per shard the builder's time is `idle + linger + build ≈ 1000 ms/s` and the sender's is `http`, since the two overlap.
| `flusher_idle_ms_total`                    | Milliseconds the queue-mode flushers spent waiting for a sample with an empty queue. Idle fraction: `rate(flusher_idle_ms_total) / (1000 × writer.shards)`. Near 1 with a rising `samples_dropped_queue_full_total` means arrivals are bursty, not that the flusher is slow.
|===

[#operations-episode-accounting]
==== Accounting for an outage

The plugin's counters cover only samples that reached `store()`.
When `store()` slows, for example while a shard spills to disk, Horizon's default ring-buffer writer discards what its writer threads cannot hand over, and those samples never reach the plugin.
OpenNMS counts them in the Dropwizard meter `ring-buffer.dropped-samples`, exported over JMX in the `org.opennms.timeseries` domain (its `Count` attribute).
Read it alongside `samples_written_total` and the `samples_dropped_*_total` counters: only the pair accounts for an episode.
On the plugin side, the early sign is a rising `store_call_duration_ms_total / store_calls_total`.
See <<overflow-caller-cost,What an outage costs the writer threads>> for why a spill slows `store()`, and how long it lasts under each drain policy.

=== Pipeline state

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Gauge                              | What it shows

| `queue_depth_high_water`                   | Maximum `queue_depth` observed since activation. Shows a queue that fills and empties between two reads of `queue_depth`.
| `queue_depth`                      | Current in-memory queue occupancy, summed over shards. Reads 0 for any shard that is spilling, since a shard's memory queue is empty while its bucket is non-empty.
| `overflow_pending_samples`         | Samples on disk past the checkpoint, summed over shards — the backlog a backend outage has built up. Divided by the drain rate, this is how long recovery will take.
| `overflow_pending_samples_shard_<i>` | The same per shard. Registered for every shard when a disk tier is configured, because hash skew fills one bucket while its siblings idle, and the total hides that.
| `overflow_bytes`                   | Bytes every shard's bucket occupies on disk. Against `overflow.max-size-bytes` this is how much outage headroom is left.
| `overflow_recovering_shards`       | Shards currently draining a backlog off disk. 1 of 4 is usually hash skew — one hot series set — where 4 of 4 is a backend that went away.
| `overflow_oldest_pending_age_ms`   | How long the oldest sample the backend has not taken has been waiting. **This is the recovery alert.** Depth says how much is queued, which is only meaningful against the drain rate; this says how far behind the tier is, in the unit the latency budget is written in. Correct from the moment a recovered bucket opens, before anything has been drained.
| `http_in_flight`                   | Running + queued HTTP requests at the dispatcher.
|===

| `http_writes_successful_total`     | 2xx responses.
| `http_writes_failed_total`         | Non-2xx + transport failures.
| `http_writes_4xx_total`, `http_writes_5xx_total`, `http_writes_transport_total` | Failed writes by cause, as request counts; see the write-path table above.
| `http_write_duration_bucket_le_*`          | Cumulative duration buckets; see the write-path table above.

=== Other

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Counter                            | What it counts

| `delete_noop_total`                | `delete(Metric)` calls (Remote Write has no delete semantic).
| `metadata_denylist_blocked_total`  | Metadata keys blocked by the built-in credential denylist.
| `metadata_series_emitted_total`    | Metadata samples (`onms_resource_attr`, `onms_resource_category`, `onms_resource_info`, `onms_resource_ifspeed`) handed to the pipeline. See <<resource-metadata>>.
| `metadata_attrs_dropped_total`     | Attributes past `metadata.attr-budget`, per emission. A steadily rising value names a resource type whose attributes outgrow the budget.
| `metadata_resources`               | Gauge: resources the write path currently remembers metadata for.
| `find_metrics_enrichment_batches_total` | Metadata queries `findMetrics` issued to restore attributes from the rows and the speed from the gauge, two per `read.discovery-batch-size` resources.
| `wal_replay_samples_total`         | Samples recovered from the disk tier at startup, summed over shards. They ship before anything offered after the restart. Approximate by a few samples either way — it sums the per-segment index counts, which over-count a segment spanning the checkpoint and under-count one whose index lagged its last append. Read it as "recovery found work", not as an accounting input; it is deliberately not a term in the reconciliation identity.
|===

== Recommended alerts

These are starting points — tune to your deployment's noise floor.

[cols="3,5",options="header"]
|===
| Alert idea                                              | Sketch

| Drop rate jumps                                         | `rate(samples_dropped_5xx_total[5m]) + rate(samples_dropped_transport_total[5m]) > 0` for 10m
| Disk tier filling up                                    | `overflow_bytes / on() (overflow_max_size_bytes_constant) > 0.8` for 15m
| Spilling at all                                         | `rate(samples_spilled_total[5m]) > 0` for 15m — the backend has not kept up for a quarter of an hour
| Recovery falling behind the budget                      | `overflow_oldest_pending_age_ms > 300000` for 10m — the oldest unwritten sample is more than five minutes old
| Queue full (no-WAL deployments)                         | `rate(samples_dropped_queue_full_total[5m]) > 0`
| `job="opennms"` cardinality exploded                    | `rate(samples_unparseable_resource_id_total[1h]) > 0` — parser regression or new resourceId shape
| Attributes silently truncated                           | `rate(metadata_attrs_dropped_total[1h]) > 0`. A resource type carries more attributes than `metadata.attr-budget`; raise the budget or add a column.
| Endpoint persistently unhappy                           | `rate(http_writes_failed_total[5m]) / rate(http_writes_successful_total[5m]) > 0.1`
| TLS skip-verify left on in production                   | log-based — the plugin emits a WARN every hour
|===

== Tuning `writer.shards`

The default (`1`) is the classic serialized pipeline. Raise it when the
queue depth grows under bursty collection while the backend is healthy —
the signature of the single-flusher throughput ceiling — or when one
slow batch visibly stalls the drain.

* Watch `shard_skew_pct` (registered when `writer.shards > 1`; 100 =
  balanced, N×100 = everything on one shard). Persistent skew means hot
  series dominate one shard; more shards won't help that shard, but the
  others stay unaffected — worst case equals the old single-flusher
  behavior for the hot shard only.
* Scale `queue.capacity` with the shard count: validation requires
  `queue.capacity / writer.shards ≥ batch.size`, and each shard's burst
  absorption is its slice of the total.
* The backend sees up to N concurrent streams; mind per-tenant ingest
  limits (Mimir, VictoriaMetrics cluster). N is capped at
  `http.max-connections`.
* Each shard owns its own overflow bucket, so sharding and durability combine — the pre-0.8.0 rule that a WAL forced `writer.shards = 1` is gone
  (the WAL is a single ordered log).
* Each shard runs two threads, `prometheus-remote-writer-flusher-<i>`
  (builder: drains the queue, builds the payload) and
  `prometheus-remote-writer-flusher-<i>-sender` (one request in flight).
  Building the next request overlaps the previous round-trip, so a
  shard's cycle is the larger of build and round-trip rather than their
  sum. While a shard's sender is blocked the shard holds two built
  batches beyond its queue slice (the handoff and the batch in the
  builder's hand), so the shutdown drain and the memory budget count
  `queue.capacity / writer.shards + 2 × batch.size` samples per shard.
* Shards deliver their ceiling only with full batches. With the queue
  usually empty each flusher wakes on the first sample and sends what is
  there; at four shards on an 11,000-device fleet that was 3,300
  requests/s of 19 samples, 40 µs of flusher time per sample against
  5 µs at a full batch. Set `batch.linger-ms` (50–200 ms) so flushers
  wait for batches to fill; `http_writes_successful_total` should fall
  by one to two orders of magnitude and
  `samples_written_total / http_writes_successful_total` approach
  `batch.size` under load.
* A full shard does not block samples bound for other shards. Under
  `queue.store-policy = partial` a `store()` call is attempted sample by
  sample; refused samples are counted and the call throws once at the end
  with `refused R of N sample(s)` in the message, so a call can be
  partially accepted and still throw. A caller that retries the whole call
  on exception (Horizon's offheap writer) would re-send the accepted
  samples on every retry, which is why `auto`, the default, gives that
  writer `all-or-nothing`: the call is refused whole, without enqueuing
  anything, when any shard lacks room for its share; the room check and the
  enqueue run under one lock so concurrent writer threads cannot slip a
  partial accept through. Same-value duplicates
  are a no-op on Prometheus, Mimir and VictoriaMetrics either way.

== Log levels

Set in `etc/org.ops4j.pax.logging.cfg` or via the Karaf shell (`log:set DEBUG org.opennms.plugins.prometheus.remotewriter`).

A stock OpenNMS Horizon sets `log4j2.logger.opennms.level = WARN` in `etc/org.ops4j.pax.logging.cfg`.
That covers the plugin's package, so none of its `INFO` lines appear by default, including the `write path:` line.
To see them, run this in the Karaf shell:

[source,console]
----
log:set INFO org.opennms.plugins.prometheus.remotewriter
----

Or add these lines to `etc/org.ops4j.pax.logging.cfg`:

[source,properties]
----
log4j2.logger.prw.name = org.opennms.plugins.prometheus.remotewriter
log4j2.logger.prw.level = INFO
----

[cols="1,4",options="header"]
|===
| Level | What you see

| `INFO`  | Plugin lifecycle (start / stop), config-change diff, the `write path:` line naming the resolved shards, queue, batch, linger and overflow settings, overflow bucket recovery at start (`shard N: recovered ... pending sample(s)`), a bucket draining back to empty (`overflow bucket is empty again`), `wire.protocol-version=2` startup WARN message.
| `WARN`  | 4xx response bodies (truncated), `instance.id` unset on startup, unknown `labels.copy` source, WAL torn-tail truncation, `tls.insecure-skip-verify=true` reminder, a shard starting to spill to disk (`shard N is spilling to disk`), an overflow drain that waited its full bound for the sender (`the overflow drain waited ...`). That last line is expected while a write is retrying against a slow backend. It points at a stuck drain only when `http_writes_successful_total` and the dropped-write counters are all flat.
| `DEBUG` | Per-batch flush results, retry timing, label-pipeline
            diff for the first sample after a config reload. Verbose —
            use sparingly.
|===

== The disk overflow tier

Each shard has a memory queue and, behind it, a durable bucket on disk. In
normal running only the queue is used. When the queue fills, the shard's
whole backlog moves into its bucket and every further sample for that shard
goes to disk until the bucket has drained — so a full queue spills rather
than refusing, and nothing is lost while the bucket has room.

The backlog moves rather than staying put because a queue fills from the
front: at the moment of the spill, memory holds the *older* samples. Sending
the newly-spilled one first would deliver a series out of order. Moving the
backlog also keeps the memory queue empty for the whole spilling period,
which is why every batch sent during an outage is a disk batch — and a disk
batch that the backend refuses is retried, never dropped.

Set `overflow.max-size-bytes = 0` to switch the tier off. A full queue then
refuses with a `StorageException`, which is how the plugin behaved before
0.8.0.

=== Sizing the tier

The budget is a stated outage tolerance. Divide it by the offered byte rate:

----
outage_window = overflow.max-size-bytes / (samples_per_second × bytes_per_sample)
----

`bytes_per_sample` depends on your label set, because the bucket stores every sample's metric name and labels uncompressed.
On an OpenNMS fleet of SNMP interface data under the 0.8 label set it measured about 340 bytes, so at 64,000 samples/sec the default 4 GiB buys about 3 to 4 minutes.
Halving the offered rate doubles the window.
Measure your own figure from `overflow_bytes` and `overflow_pending_samples`; <<overflow-sizing,Sizing the disk tier>> explains how, including the baseline to subtract.

The budget is split evenly across `writer.shards` at startup, so hash skew across shards applies to disk exactly as it does to memory.
The hottest shard exhausts its slice while its siblings idle, and that slice sets the window.
Watch `overflow_pending_samples_shard_<i>`, not just the total.

`overflow.full` decides what happens when a bucket does run out. `refuse`
(the default) pushes the decision back to OpenNMS and counts the loss in
`samples_dropped_overflow_full_total`. `drop-oldest` evicts the oldest whole
segment and keeps accepting, counting what it discarded in
`samples_evicted_overflow_total` — newest-data-wins, chosen deliberately.

== Capacity sizing

These are rough rules-of-thumb, not hard requirements. Measure your
deployment.

[cols="3,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| Sample rate           | Settings                                  | Notes

| < 5k samples/sec      | defaults                                  | `queue.capacity = 10000` and the default 4 GiB tier are plenty.
| 5k–25k samples/sec    | `queue.capacity = 50000`, `batch.size = 2000`, `http.max-connections = 32` | Raise `overflow.max-size-bytes` if you want the same outage window at the higher rate.
| 25k+ samples/sec      | `writer.shards = 4`, `queue.capacity = 40000`, `batch.size = 1000`, `batch.linger-ms = 100` | Single-process Karaf is the bottleneck before the wire. Size the tier from the outage window you actually want, not from a round number.
|===

The metadata series add about `resources × 6 ÷ cadence in seconds` samples per second (about 1,500 for 220,000 resources at the default 15 minutes) and, more importantly, about six series per resource on the backend; see <<resource-metadata>> for the arithmetic and the backend limits to check.

== Self-monitoring with the plugin itself

While the plugin is active, every counter and gauge in the tables above
is registered as an MBean in the Karaf JVM under the domain
`org.opennms.plugins.prometheus.remotewriter`. The MBean name carries
the metric name and its kind, for example
`org.opennms.plugins.prometheus.remotewriter:name=samples_written_total,type=counters`
and `...:name=queue_depth,type=gauges`. Counters expose the attribute
`Count`, gauges `Value`. Several `_total` names are read live from the
component that owns the state rather than counted in the registry, for
example `http_writes_successful_total`, `http_writes_failed_total`,
`samples_dropped_queue_full_total` and `flusher_idle_ms_total`, and
therefore appear under `type=gauges` with `Value`. Do not derive the type
from the name; the exporter rule below covers both shapes.
The MBeans are removed when the plugin stops and re-created on a
configuration reload.

A https://github.com/prometheus/jmx_exporter[JMX exporter] against the
Karaf JVM turns them into time series. One rule covers all of them:

[source,yaml]
----
rules:
  - pattern: 'org.opennms.plugins.prometheus.remotewriter<name=http_write_duration_bucket_le_(.+), type=gauges><>Value'
    name: prometheus_remote_writer_http_write_duration_ms_bucket
    labels:
      le: '$1'
    type: COUNTER
  - pattern: 'org.opennms.plugins.prometheus.remotewriter<name=(.+), type=(counters|gauges)><>(Count|Value)'
    name: prometheus_remote_writer_$1
    type: UNTYPED
----

Write those series into the same backend the plugin writes to and one
Grafana dashboard shows both the OpenNMS data and the plugin's own
health. `opennms:prometheus-writer-stats` in the Karaf shell remains the
manual way to read the same values.
